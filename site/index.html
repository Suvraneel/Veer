Private by design

Your files stay on your Mac.

A converter in a browser tab has to take your file away to work on it. Radlet does the work where the file already is.

A converter website

upload → wait → download

  • Your file goes to a server you don't control, and you trust it's deleted afterwards.
  • Everything travels both ways, so a big video can take longer to move than to convert.
  • Free tiers often cap file sizes or count conversions, then ask for an account.
  • A batch means one tab per file, or a bigger plan.

Radlet

drag → flick → done

  • Files are read and written on this Mac and never sent anywhere.
  • Works offline, at your Mac's own speed, even on a plane.
  • No caps, no counter, no account and no watermark.
  • Drop a whole folder: each file runs on its own, and every job can be undone.

Radlet goes online for two things: checking for updates, and downloading FFmpeg if you say yes. It has no accounts or telemetry, and the source is public, so you can check.

Loadouts

Your loadout, one flick away.

Like a game loadout, but for files. Save any tool's settings as a loadout from its window, or chain steps in a small text file. Hold Control while dragging and they're on the wheel.

  • One decode and one encode per chain, so steps never compress twice.
  • Your shortcuts from the Shortcuts app can join the wheel too.
  • With Apple Intelligence, describe one in a sentence, like “under 2 MB, as PDF”, and review the steps it drafts.
  • Share loadouts and skins as a link or a file. Ready-made ones are in the gallery: one click opens them in Radlet.
  • Run them on watch folders. Passwords never go into a loadout.
web-ready-image.tomlLoadout
name = "Web-ready image"
description = "Longest side 2048 px, WebP, no metadata"

[[steps]]
op = "resize"
max = 2048

[[steps]]
op = "convert"
to = "webp"
quality = 80

[[steps]]
op = "strip_metadata"

Command line & AI agents

From the terminal, or from Claude.

Almost every tool on the wheel is a radlet command, ready for scripts and Shortcuts. radlet mcp lets Claude and other AI apps convert, compress and clean your files on your own Mac.

  • JSON output for scripts with --json, and an exit code from radlet check for your CI.
  • Agents get the wheel's tools: convert, compress, combine, check syntax, describe, summarize, run loadouts and more.
  • Password tools and your own shortcuts are never offered to agents.

Questions

Good to know.

Is Radlet free?

Yes, with no limits. Download it here or from GitHub. There's no account to make, no subscription, no watermark and no ads.

Is there a limit on how many files, or how big?

No allowance and no counter: drop one file or a folder of thousands, as often as you like. Radlet only turns away files too big for any Mac to handle sensibly, such as pictures over 40,000 pixels a side, and says which file and why.

Does it upload my files anywhere?

No. Converting, compressing, text recognition, background removal, face hiding and translation all run on your Mac, using Apple's own frameworks where they help. Radlet only goes online to check for updates and, if you say yes, to download FFmpeg; your files never leave your Mac.

Can it convert HEIC photos to JPG?

Yes: drag the photos, hold Shift and drop on JPEG. The JPEGs appear next to the originals. It also turns PDFs into Word, logos into SVG drawings, Word documents into PDFs, CSV into Excel, MOV into MP4 and a lot more.

Do I need to install FFmpeg?

No. Everyday audio and video work out of the box: MP4 and MOV, sound to M4A, WAV or AIFF, trimming, joining and compressing. A few things need FFmpeg, such as MP3, WebM or MKV output and making GIFs. Those slots show a small badge, and choosing one offers to install it: with Homebrew if you have it, otherwise Radlet downloads its own build (about 55 MB) and checks it before use.

Does it use Apple Intelligence?

Where it helps, and only on your Mac. With Apple Intelligence, Radlet can describe a picture as alt text, summarize a document, name files by what's in them and draft a loadout from a sentence. Everything else works without it.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. Translating documents needs macOS 26, and the Apple Intelligence tools need macOS 26 on Apple silicon.

Why does macOS warn me the first time I open it?

Radlet isn't notarized by Apple yet. On macOS 15 or later, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On macOS 14, Control-click Radlet in Applications and choose Open. You only do this once.

Something's wrong, or missing. Where do I say?

Open an issue on GitHub with what you tried and what happened, or the format or tool you'd like. If a job failed, Troubleshooting → Copy Diagnostics in the menu bar copies the details for your report, with no file names or paths.

Why “Radlet”

Radial+-let=Radlet

Radial, because it's a wheel. In German and Dutch, Rad even means wheel.

-let, because it's a small one, as in droplet or booklet: it opens under your pointer and gets out of the way.

More importantly, rad is slang for awesome.

The wheel tries to live up to that.
